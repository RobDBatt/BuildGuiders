## CRITICAL: Fire TV Stick Lite is 1080p ONLY

**The Fire TV Stick Lite is 1080p maximum, not 4K.** Do not write that it supports 4K, 4K HDR, or Dolby Vision. Any article about a Fire TV Stick Lite that claims 4K output is factually incorrect.

The Lite model is identifiable by its smaller remote without volume/power buttons and lower price point (~$17–20).

**No Fire TV Stick has built-in Ethernet.** The Fire TV Cube supports Ethernet via optional adapter only.

## Audio Output

- **Dolby Atmos:** Supported on 4K Max, 4K, Cube (via DD+ compressed) — NOT TrueHD
- **DTS:** DTS Passthrough on 4K Max and Cube
- **Fire TV Stick Lite:** No Dolby Atmos support
- **eARC passthrough:** Not applicable — these are source devices

## Ecosystem

- **OS:** Fire OS (Android-based, Amazon skin)
- **CEC name:** Standard HDMI CEC (Amazon calls this "Fire TV CEC" or just "HDMI CEC")
- **Alexa:** All models have Alexa voice built into remote
- **Local storage playback:** Limited support via Kodi sideloading; no native USB media (except Cube which has USB-A)

## App Availability

Available: Netflix, Disney+, Max, Prime Video, Hulu, Peacock, ESPN+, YouTube, Paramount+
Not pre-installed but available: Apple TV+ (must install from app store)

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Never write that Fire TV Stick Lite supports 4K.** It is 1080p only.
2. **Never write that Fire TV Stick Lite supports Dolby Vision.** Not supported.
3. **Never say Fire TV sticks have Ethernet.** No built-in Ethernet on any stick model.
4. **Never say Fire TV Atmos output is TrueHD lossless.** It is Dolby Digital Plus (lossy).
5. **Do not generalize "Fire TV Stick" specs across the entire lineup.** The Lite, 4K, and 4K Max have meaningfully different specs.
