# Deck stain sources register (checked 2026-11-11)

The figures and statements the deck-stain guides and the planned
`/deck-stain-problems` diagnoser may use, each one opened and read at its source.
Anything not listed here has no checked source. Write it as unavailable (Form 3)
or leave it out.

## USDA Forest Products Laboratory, *Wood Handbook* (FPL-GTR-282, 2021), Chapter 16 "Finishing Wood" (C. G. Hunt)

https://research.fs.usda.gov/treesearch/62269. This is a named independent
source.

- Keeping wood at or below about 20% moisture content in service is "the single
  most important step" for finish performance.
- Treated lumber is sometimes still above fibre saturation when delivered. You
  can tell because it is exceptionally heavy, or water comes out when a small
  piece is squeezed. "Nothing will coat wet wood well": wait for it to dry, use a
  penetrating finish, or buy KDAT.
- If a semitransparent finish appears shiny an hour after application, it has
  not penetrated. Remove the excess, or the thin film "may crack and peel within
  a year or two." Even on weathered wood, a second coat may not absorb.
- Film-forming finishes on decks: "extremely challenging" to get them to
  adhere, and "peeling is very common". Penetrating finishes are recommended.
  Solid-colour stains form films and peel like paint.
- Limit stain to what the surface can absorb. A brush is best; rollers and
  sprayers may put too much on horizontal surfaces.
- One coat of stain on new smooth wood lasts only 2 to 4 years. Later coats
  last longer as the wood ages.
- Finish that fails on weathered wood often has wood fibres attached to the
  back of the film.
- Power washing erodes earlywood faster than latewood, leaving ridges.
  Low-pressure washing is less damaging. *No PSI figure is given.*
- Sodium percarbonate (an oxygen bleach) is the active ingredient in many deck
  cleaners. Oxalic-acid brighteners remove extractive bleed and iron stains but
  not mildew.
- Cloths wet with oil-based stain are particularly susceptible to spontaneous
  combustion. Immerse them in water in a sealed, water-filled metal container.

## Behr, BEHR PREMIUM Semi-Transparent Waterproofing Stain & Sealer (product page)

https://www.behr.com/products/wood-stains/behr-premium-semi-transparent-waterproofing-stain-and-sealer.
Read in Chrome (WebFetch is refused with a 403). Manufacturer source.

- Spread rate 250–300 sq ft (first coat), 500–600 sq ft (second coat). "2 Thin
  Coats Required."
- Must apply between 40–90°F. No rain expected within 24 hours. The surface
  must be completely dry.
- "Over application of stain will lead to surface failure including peeling and
  cracking."
- No. 63 cleaner MUST be used after No. 64 stripper, to clean, brighten and
  neutralize. On new wood, No. 63 removes mill glaze.
- 1–2 hours before recoat; 72 hours to full cure.
- Warranty: 6 years horizontal, 8 vertical. Void if not properly applied to a
  properly prepared surface. Excludes labour and incidental/consequential
  damages. Remedy is replacement product or a refund. Colour fading and slight
  greying "may occur".

## Ready Seal, generic brochure (EN r4)

Hosted by a distributor:
https://dfm.fidelio.ca/Public/img/catalog/7450_403575_ReadySeal_Generic_Broc_EN_r4.pdf.
Manufacturer source.

- Approximate coverage: spruce fence 150 sq ft/gal, redwood deck 175 sq ft/gal.
  "Actual coverage will vary."
- Darkest when first applied; reaches its true colour in about 14 days.
- Marketed as "no laps, no runs, no streaks" and "no back brushing".

## Checked and not usable

- **Defy Extreme coverage.** Retail listings give 100–150 sq ft/gal for two
  coats, 150, and 150–300. No manufacturer page was found. The site's old
  "150–200" figure matched none of them.
- **Defy "zinc nanoparticles."** These appear only on third-party listings.
- **Restore-A-Deck.** restoreadeck.com is a parked domain. The coverage figures
  are on retailer pages only, and the old "300–600 sq ft" stripper-kit figure
  matched none of them.
- **Stripper dwell times.** Behr's label excerpt gives temperatures, not a
  time. Third-party figures contradict each other.
- **"Moisture below 15%" and "PT lumber holds 20–30%."** No source found. The
  *Wood Handbook* gives about 20% (in service) and the squeeze/weight test
  instead.
