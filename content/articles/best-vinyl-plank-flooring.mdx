"Luxury vinyl plank" now covers everything from a $1.50 builder special to a $5 designer floor, and the marketing on the box rarely tells you which one you're holding. The good news: two numbers and one question sort the entire category. Get those right and the brand almost picks itself. Here's how the real contenders stack up, and which floor fits the room you're actually doing.

## First question: SPC or WPC core?

Almost every rigid plank worth buying is built on one of two cores, and this is the choice that decides how the floor feels and where it belongs.

**SPC (stone-plastic composite)** is limestone powder, PVC, and stabilizers pressed into a dense, hard core. It shrugs off dents from heavy furniture and pet claws, stays stable in temperature swings, and runs thinner (about 4 to 8 mm) while staying rigid. It's the right call for kitchens, entryways, finished basements, and any high-traffic or pet-heavy room. The trade-off is that it's harder and a touch louder underfoot.

**WPC (wood-plastic composite)** mixes wood pulp, PVC, and a foaming agent, which adds air pockets. That makes it softer, warmer, and quieter to walk on, but less dent-resistant, and it's usually thicker (5 to 12 mm) to stay stable. It's the comfort pick for bedrooms, living rooms, and home offices where softness underfoot beats maximum toughness.

**Rule of thumb:** busy and wet rooms want SPC; bedrooms and lounges can enjoy WPC. When in doubt, SPC is the safer all-house default, and it's what most 2026 rigid-core lines use.

## Second number: the wear layer, in mil

The wear layer is the clear top coat that takes all the abuse, measured in mil (thousandths of an inch). **Do not confuse it with overall plank thickness** — a thick plank with a thin wear layer still scratches.

- **6 mil** — builder-grade and rental flips. Fine for light traffic, not for a busy household.
- **12 mil** — the residential sweet spot. Handles any normal home and light commercial use.
- **20 mil** — pets, kids, rolling chairs, and high-traffic entryways. This is the upgrade that pays for itself if you have a dog or a busy front door.
- **22 to 30 mil** — true commercial duty; overkill for most homes.

If you remember one thing from this guide: **match the wear layer to your traffic, not your budget.** A 20 mil plank in the entry and a 12 mil in the bedrooms is smarter than one mid-grade floor everywhere.

## Where you buy decides what you get

The three best-known names are each tied to one store, which matters more than the marketing admits:

- **Lifeproof — Home Depot exclusive.** Home Depot's in-house brand, roughly $2 to $5 per square foot, with an attached pad on most lines and a genuinely easy click-lock install. A strong, widely stocked default if you're already shopping there.
- **SmartCore — Lowe's exclusive.** Made by COREtec (now Shaw), with a rigid SPC core, GreenGuard Gold certification, and a lifetime residential warranty, often $1.50 to $4 per square foot. The value champion at Lowe's.
- **COREtec — flooring dealers.** The brand that invented rigid-core LVP and still sets the bar, sold through flooring stores rather than big boxes. Premium price, premium visuals, attached cork backing on many lines.

Because those three are store-only, you won't find them on Amazon — so if you're ordering online, look instead for a **rigid SPC plank with a 20 mil wear layer** from a reputable seller, or a known online value brand like **Lucida**. The spec sheet matters more than the logo here: SPC core, the wear layer you need, and a real warranty.

## Best for renters and small rooms: peel-and-stick

If you can't modify the space or you're only doing a closet, laundry, or powder room, skip the floating floor entirely. **Peel-and-stick vinyl plank** needs no underlayment, no click-lock, and no transitions — it goes down in an afternoon and comes up later without damage. It won't match rigid core for durability or underfoot feel, but for the right small job it's the honest, cheap answer.

## Don't skip the underlayment and the install kit

The planks are only half the floor. Two things people forget on install day:

- **Underlayment.** If your plank does **not** have an attached pad, you need a 3-in-1 underlayment for the moisture barrier and sound dampening, especially over concrete. If it **does** have a pad, don't add more — doubling up makes the floor spongy and can void the warranty.
- **The install kit.** A floating floor needs a **tapping block, a pull bar** for the final row, and **spacers** for the expansion gap at the walls. A **plank cutter** turns hours of score-and-snap into clean, fast crosscuts. None of it is expensive, and all of it is the difference between a tight floor and one with creeping gaps.

## FAQ

**Is SPC or WPC vinyl plank better?**
Neither is universally better. SPC is harder and more dent- and temperature-resistant — best for kitchens, basements, entryways, and pets. WPC is softer, warmer, and quieter underfoot — best for bedrooms and living rooms. For a whole-house floor, SPC is the safer default.

**What wear layer do I need with a dog?**
Go with a 20 mil wear layer for pets and high traffic. A 12 mil layer is fine for normal residential rooms, but claws and heavy use scratch thinner top coats faster.

**Why can't I find Lifeproof or SmartCore online?**
They're store exclusives — Lifeproof is sold only at Home Depot and SmartCore only at Lowe's. If you're buying online, shop by spec instead: a rigid SPC core with the wear layer your rooms need.

**Is all vinyl plank flooring waterproof?**
Rigid-core LVP (SPC and WPC) is 100% waterproof through the plank, which is why it works in kitchens, baths, and basements. The seams and subfloor still need proper prep, but spills won't ruin the planks.

**Do I need underlayment under vinyl plank?**
Only if the plank doesn't already have a pad attached. If it does, skip separate underlayment — never stack two pads. Over concrete, always confirm you have a moisture barrier, whether attached or separate.
