## What to look for

A bathroom is not a hard room to paint. It is a hard room to stay painted. Steam condenses on whatever surface in the room is coldest — usually the ceiling right over the shower — and sits there for an hour after everyone has left. Paint that is not built for that grows mildew inside the film, and once it is in the film, wiping the surface does nothing at all. You are repainting.

So two things on the label matter. One is a mildewcide in the can, which you will see as "mold and mildew resistant" or a kitchen-and-bath designation. That additive works on the dried film; it does not sterilise the room. The other is whether the finish can actually be scrubbed without burnishing — going shiny in patches where the cloth went.

Sheen is where most bathrooms go wrong. The old advice was semi-gloss everywhere, and semi-gloss does shed water. It also throws the bathroom's overhead light straight down the wall and shows every roller lap and every trowel mark in the drywall. Satin holds out nearly as well and hides far more. Save the semi-gloss for the trim and the vanity.

Before you buy any of this, be honest about the fan. If the mirror is still fogged ten minutes after a shower, the exhaust fan is undersized or vented into the attic instead of outside, and no paint on this list will fix that. Mildew keeps coming back on a wall that never dries.

Then size the order. A gallon of quality interior paint covers 350 to 400 square feet in one coat. A 5-by-8 bathroom is roughly 200 square feet of wall once you take out the door and the window, so one gallon gets you two coats with enough left for touch-ups. The ceiling is another 40 square feet and usually wants a different sheen, which makes it a separate quart rather than the tail end of the same can.

## Zinsser Perma-White

This is the one to buy if mildew is the actual problem — if you have scrubbed a ceiling and watched the spots come back. Perma-White carries a mildewcide in the film and Rust-Oleum guarantees it against mold and mildew growth for five years, which is a specific commitment rather than the vague "mildew resistant" on most cans.

It is also self-priming over sound previously painted surfaces, so a clean, dull wall takes it directly. That saves a whole coat and half a day. It comes in flat, eggshell, satin and semi-gloss, and it is genuinely scrubbable.

The limitation is colour. Perma-White is a white base that tints to light shades only, so white, off-white and pale colours are the whole range. If you have picked a deep green for the powder room, this paint cannot make it, and that is the end of the conversation.

## Benjamin Moore Aura Bath & Spa

The reason to spend here is that it is matte and still belongs in a bathroom. That combination normally does not exist: matte paint is porous, holds moisture and burnishes the moment you wipe it. Benjamin Moore's ColorLock film is dense enough to take a cleaning cloth and shed condensation while still reading as flat on the wall.

If you want the soft, chalky look that flat gives a small room and you were told you could not have it over a shower, this is the exception. It is also a zero-VOC formula by Benjamin Moore's own measure, which is worth something in a room with a closed door and one small fan.

Two catches. It is matte only, so your trim and vanity need a different product anyway. And it is the most expensive paint here by a wide margin — a premium you are paying specifically for the finish, not for better moisture performance than Perma-White.

## Sherwin-Williams Emerald Interior Acrylic

Emerald is the answer when the colour matters more than anything else on this page. It covers about 400 square feet per gallon, hides in two coats over most existing colours, resists mildew on the film, and comes in the full Sherwin-Williams fan deck in matte through semi-gloss. Nothing else here gives you both the colour range and the washability.

It is a premium paint at a premium price, but Sherwin-Williams runs store sales often and hard, and buying Emerald on a sale week closes most of the gap to the mid-tier.

Use it for a bathroom you care how it looks in. Use Perma-White for a bathroom with a mildew history, because the five-year guarantee is a better tool for that specific job than a better paint with a generic claim.

## Behr Premium Plus

Behr is the pick for a rental, a guest bath nobody showers in, or a refresh before a listing. It carries a mildew-resistant finish, sits on the shelf at Home Depot with no pro-store trip, and the paint-and-primer formula goes over previously painted walls fine.

Expect to spend a third coat on a real colour change. Behr's coverage is closer to 350 square feet per gallon and it is thicker under the roller than Emerald, so an overloaded roller leaves texture behind. Load it lighter than feels right and keep a wet edge.

It is not the paint for a steam shower or a bathroom that has already grown mildew once. For everything else, it is a lot of paint for the money.

## What to skip

Ordinary flat wall paint with no bath designation. It is the cheapest-looking failure in this room: porous, holds the moisture against the drywall, and cannot be cleaned without leaving a shiny patch where you cleaned it. It will mildew, and you will be back inside two years.

Oil-based paint, which still gets recommended for bathrooms out of habit. It is hard and washable and it yellows — badly, and fastest in rooms that do not get daylight, which describes most bathrooms. It also moves less than latex as the room heats and cools, so it cracks at the seams.

And skip painting over existing mildew. Every product here resists growth on its own film; none of them kills what is underneath. Clean the spots first, let the wall dry, then paint.
