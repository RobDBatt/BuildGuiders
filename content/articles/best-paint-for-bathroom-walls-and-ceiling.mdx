## What to Look for in Paint for Bathroom Walls and Ceiling

Choosing the right paint for your bathroom is crucial due to the unique challenges of high humidity, frequent temperature changes, and potential for mold and mildew growth. The paint you select needs to stand up to moisture, be easy to clean, and maintain its appearance over time. Prioritizing paints specifically formulated for bathrooms can save you from premature peeling, blistering, and unsightly mildew stains.

The most important factor is moisture and mildew resistance. Bathroom paints often contain mildewcides that inhibit fungal growth, a common problem in steamy environments. Look for formulations that explicitly state "mildew-resistant" or "mold and mildew proof." Beyond this, durability and washability are key. Bathroom surfaces often need more frequent cleaning than other rooms, so a paint that can withstand scrubbing without losing its finish is essential. This often correlates with higher sheen levels, but modern matte or flat bathroom paints are also available with enhanced durability.

Finally, consider the sheen level and Volatile Organic Compounds (VOCs). Higher sheens like semi-gloss or satin are traditionally recommended for bathrooms because their smoother, tighter surface is less permeable to moisture and easier to wipe clean. However, some manufacturers now offer eggshell or even matte finishes with advanced moisture-resistant technologies. Low-VOC or zero-VOC paints are a good choice for bathrooms, especially smaller ones, as they contribute to better indoor air quality during and after application.

## What to Skip

Avoid using standard flat or matte interior paints that are not specifically formulated for bathrooms. While they might be suitable for living rooms or bedrooms, their porous surface can absorb moisture, leading to blistering, peeling, and rapid mold and mildew growth in a high-humidity bathroom. Also, steer clear of cheap, general-purpose paints that don't explicitly mention mildew resistance or enhanced washability. These paints often lack the necessary additives and durable binders to withstand the unique challenges of a bathroom environment, leading to premature failure and the need for repainting much sooner than expected.
