## What to Look for in Paint for Bathroom Walls and Ceiling

Choosing the right paint for your bathroom is crucial due to the unique challenges of high humidity, moisture, and temperature fluctuations. Standard interior paints often aren't formulated to withstand these conditions, leading to issues like peeling, blistering, and mildew growth. The primary factors to consider are moisture resistance, mildew resistance, durability, and the appropriate sheen.

Moisture resistance helps prevent the paint film from breaking down when exposed to steam and splashes, which is common in bathrooms. Mildew resistance is often achieved through fungicides added to the paint formula, inhibiting the growth of mold and mildew that thrive in damp environments. Durability, particularly washability, is also important, as bathroom surfaces may need frequent cleaning to remove soap scum or other residues without damaging the paint. Finally, the paint's sheen affects both its appearance and its performance; higher sheens generally offer better moisture resistance and easier cleaning.

## What to Skip

Avoid using flat or matte finishes that are not specifically formulated for bathrooms. While aesthetically pleasing in other rooms, traditional flat paints are highly porous, making them difficult to clean and prone to absorbing moisture, which can lead to mildew growth and staining. Also, steer clear of standard interior paints that don't explicitly mention moisture or mildew resistance. While they might be cheaper upfront, they will likely fail prematurely in a bathroom environment, leading to more work and expense down the line. Oil-based paints, while durable, are generally not recommended for bathrooms due to their strong odor, longer drying times, tendency to yellow over time, and difficult cleanup.
