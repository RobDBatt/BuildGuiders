## What to Look for in Bathroom Paint

Choosing paint for a bathroom means considering the unique challenges of a high-moisture environment. The primary concern is preventing mold and mildew growth, which thrives in damp conditions. Look for paints specifically formulated with mildewcides or antimicrobial additives to inhibit fungal growth on the paint film. These specialized paints are often labeled as "bathroom paint" or "kitchen and bath" formulas.

Beyond mildew resistance, durability and washability are important. Bathroom walls and ceilings are exposed to steam, splashes, and condensation, requiring a surface that can withstand frequent cleaning without losing its finish or integrity. Paints with a higher sheen, such as semi-gloss or satin, are generally more durable and easier to wipe clean than flat finishes. However, some modern matte or eggshell bathroom paints are engineered to offer similar moisture resistance and scrubbability.

Finally, consider the paint's ability to withstand humidity without peeling or blistering. Some paints are designed with enhanced adhesion properties for humid conditions. While not always a primary concern, low-VOC (volatile organic compound) options can improve indoor air quality, which is particularly relevant in smaller, less ventilated bathrooms.

## What to Skip

Avoid using standard flat or matte interior paints that are not specifically formulated for bathrooms. These finishes are typically more porous and can absorb moisture, making them prone to mildew growth and difficult to clean without damaging the surface. They will not hold up to the humidity and splashes common in a bathroom, leading to premature peeling, staining, and mildew. Also, steer clear of oil-based paints for bathrooms; while durable, they tend to yellow over time, have high VOCs, and are less flexible than latex paints, making them more susceptible to cracking in fluctuating temperatures. Always check for "kitchen and bath" or "mildew resistant" on the label.
