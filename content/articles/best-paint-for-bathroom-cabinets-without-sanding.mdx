## What to Look for in Paint for Bathroom Cabinets Without Sanding

Choosing paint for bathroom cabinets without sanding requires focusing on products designed for strong adhesion to existing finishes. The primary concern is how well the paint will bond to the cabinet surface, whether it is previously painted, stained, or laminated, without the mechanical etching that sanding provides. Look for paints that are explicitly labeled as self-priming or those recommended for use with a dedicated bonding primer. These formulations contain resins that grip slick surfaces.

Beyond adhesion, durability and moisture resistance are important for bathroom environments. Cabinets in bathrooms face humidity, water splashes, and frequent cleaning. An enamel finish, often found in urethane-modified alkyds or acrylic-urethanes, offers a hard, chip-resistant surface that stands up to wear. These paints also tend to have better flow and leveling properties, which helps achieve a smooth finish without brush marks. Mildew resistance is another factor, as bathrooms are prone to fungal growth. Some paints include additives to inhibit mildew, which can be beneficial for cabinet surfaces.

Consider the sheen level as well. While flat or matte finishes can hide imperfections, they are generally less durable and harder to clean than higher sheens. A satin or semi-gloss finish is often preferred for bathroom cabinets. These sheens offer increased durability, are easier to wipe down, and reflect enough light to brighten the space without being overly reflective. A higher sheen also enhances the perception of cleanliness.

## What to Skip

Avoid general-purpose interior latex paints that do not explicitly state self-priming or bonding properties. These paints are often formulated for drywall and may not adhere well to slick cabinet surfaces without extensive sanding and priming, leading to peeling or chipping. Also, skip any paint that is not designed for high-moisture environments, as it will likely fail prematurely due to humidity, steam, and water splashes. Very inexpensive paints often lack the high-quality resins and additives needed for durability and adhesion on cabinets, making them a false economy.
