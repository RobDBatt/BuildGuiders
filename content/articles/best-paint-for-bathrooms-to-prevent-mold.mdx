Bathrooms are tough on paint. The constant humidity and temperature swings create a prime environment for mold and mildew, which can quickly ruin a fresh coat and leave your walls looking stained and dingy. The right paint for a bathroom needs to resist moisture and actively inhibit mildew growth.

When you're choosing, look for water-based acrylic latex formulas that include mildew-resistant additives. Sheen matters too: satin or semi-gloss finishes are generally recommended. They create a smoother, harder surface that repels moisture and cleans easily. Flat and eggshell finishes are porous and hold onto moisture, so they are not a good choice for high-humidity areas. A gallon of quality latex paint typically covers 350 to 400 square feet per coat on smooth, sealed surfaces. If your walls are porous, expect coverage closer to 200 to 300 square feet per gallon. Most interior paint jobs need two coats for full, even coverage.

## What to Look for in Paint for Bathrooms

The primary factors for bathroom paint are moisture resistance and mildew inhibitors. Paint designed for bathrooms forms a durable film that resists water penetration, which helps prevent the paint from blistering, peeling, or cracking in humid conditions. Mildew-resistant additives are chemicals blended into the paint that actively deter mold and mildew spores from taking hold and growing on the paint surface.

Beyond these protective qualities, consider the sheen. Satin and semi-gloss paints are smoother and less porous than flat or eggshell finishes. This smooth surface makes them easier to wipe clean, which is important for removing condensation and preventing mildew buildup. Some premium paints now offer a matte finish with the same moisture and mildew resistance, giving you more aesthetic options without sacrificing performance.

## What to Skip

Skip flat or eggshell finishes for bathrooms. These porous surfaces absorb moisture and are difficult to clean, making them prone to mold and mildew. Also, avoid paints that are not specifically formulated with mildew inhibitors, even if they claim to be washable. The humidity in a bathroom demands a paint that actively fights mold, not just one that cleans up easily. Finally, do not try to apply paint too thickly, especially with products like Sherwin-Williams Duration Home. Thick coats can lead to improper drying and adhesion issues.
