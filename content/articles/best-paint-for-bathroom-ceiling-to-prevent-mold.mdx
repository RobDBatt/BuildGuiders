The ceiling over the shower is the coldest surface in the room, so that is where the steam goes. It condenses, sits there for an hour after everyone has left, and feeds whatever is living in the paint film. That is why the spots come back in the same place after you scrub them: scrubbing cleans the surface, and the growth is in the film.

A quart is usually enough. A 5-by-8 bathroom has about 40 square feet of ceiling, and the paints here cover 250 to 400 square feet per gallon depending on how porous the surface is, so two coats of a quart will do a small bathroom with paint left over.

Spend the first ten minutes on the fan, not the paint. If the mirror is still fogged ten minutes after a shower, the fan is undersized or it is venting into the attic instead of outside. No paint on this page fixes a ceiling that never dries, and the mildewcide in all of them works on the film rather than on the room.

## Zinsser Perma-White

Buy this one. Rust-Oleum guarantees the dried film against mold and mildew for five years, which is a specific commitment with a number attached rather than the "mildew resistant" that every other can claims. It is self-priming over a sound painted ceiling, so a clean, dull surface takes it directly.

Two things the label does not put on the front. **It needs two coats for the mold and mildew resistance to work as intended** — the guarantee is written against the system, not against one thin coat, and one coat is the most common way to not get what you paid for. And it keeps hardening long after it looks finished: 30 minutes to touch, 2 hours to recoat, 5 to 7 days before it will take a scrub, and 7 to 10 days to full cure. Do not wipe it in week one.

Where it runs out of road is colour. Perma-White is a white base that tints to light shades only, so white, off-white and pale colours are the whole range. On a ceiling that costs you nothing. On a wall where you have picked a deep green it ends the conversation. [Zinsser Perma-White Mold & Mildew Proof Paint (paid link)](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Zinsser+Perma-White+Bathroom+Paint&tag=buildguiders-20) comes in satin and semi-gloss as well as flat.

One claim worth being careful with, because it is quoted all over the place: Perma-White's Class 1 wet-scrub rating under ISO 11998 comes from Zinsser's own European data sheet, tested to EN 13300. It is a real standardised result, but it is the manufacturer reporting it, not an independent lab, and it is published for the European product rather than the can on a shelf in Ohio.

## Benjamin Moore Aura Bath & Spa

The reason to spend here is that it is matte and still belongs over a shower. That normally does not exist — matte is porous, holds moisture and goes shiny in patches the moment you wipe it. Benjamin Moore's Color Lock film is dense enough to take a cloth and shed condensation while still reading as flat.

If you want the soft, chalky ceiling a flat finish gives a small room, and you were told a bathroom rules it out, this is the exception. Zero VOC on the US product, which is worth something in a room with the door shut and one weak fan. Two coats, about an hour between them.

It is matte only, so your trim needs a different product anyway, and it is the most expensive paint on this page by a wide margin. You are paying that premium for the finish, not for better moisture performance than Perma-White.

## Sherwin-Williams Duration Home

This is the one to pick when the colour decides it. Duration Home carries antimicrobial agents that Sherwin-Williams says inhibit mold and mildew growth on the paint surface, it comes in the full fan deck in flat through semi-gloss, and it returns to service in about two hours.

Be clear about what it is, though. It is an interior wall paint that is rated for humid rooms, not a ceiling paint. Nothing stops you rolling it on a ceiling, and on a flat ceiling you lose the thing it is actually good at, which is wiping clean on a wall at satin or semi-gloss. If the ceiling is the only surface you are painting, this is the wrong tin to open.

## BEHR Premium Plus Interior Ceiling

The budget pick, and the right one for a textured or popcorn ceiling. Behr's flat, non-reflective sheen keeps the original acoustical properties of the ceiling, which the dense film on a bathroom-specific paint will not, and it is formulated to resist spatter, which matters when you are rolling above your head. Mildew resistance in the dried film, 250 to 400 square feet a gallon, an hour to touch and two to recoat.

Here is what I could not find, and I went looking: Behr publishes no cure-before-washing figure for this paint. Their flat interior wall paint says two weeks before you clean it with mild detergent, but that is a different product, and the ceiling page says nothing. Treat it as "longer than you think" and do not test it.

A flat finish is the trade. It is the least washable option here, and on a ceiling that is mostly fine, because nobody scrubs a ceiling — until the one in a bathroom with a bad fan, which is the whole reason you are reading this.

## What to skip

Standard ceiling paint with no mildew claim on the can. It is the cheapest-looking failure in the room and you will be back inside two years.

High gloss, however good it looks in the showroom. It throws the overhead light straight across the ceiling and shows every roller lap and every trowel mark in the drywall.

And painting over live mold. Every product here resists growth on its own film; none of them kills what is already underneath. Clean the spots, let the ceiling dry properly, then paint.

## Bottom Line

**Zinsser Perma-White**, in two coats, and leave it alone for a week. The five-year guarantee is the only promise on this page with a number attached, it primes itself, and on a ceiling that was going to be white anyway the colour limit costs you nothing.

Buy Benjamin Moore Aura Bath & Spa instead if you want a matte ceiling and will pay for it, or the Behr if the ceiling is textured and you want to keep it that way.

Then go look at the fan. A ceiling that dries in twenty minutes does not need any of this.
