The steam from daily showers and baths turns a bathroom ceiling into a prime spot for mold and mildew. A standard ceiling paint won't hold up. You need a paint formulated to resist moisture and inhibit fungal growth, or you'll be scrubbing or repainting sooner than you expect.

A gallon of quality paint typically covers about 350 to 400 square feet per coat on a smooth, sealed surface. Textured or porous ceilings will reduce that coverage. Remember that proper preparation is non-negotiable. Clean the surface thoroughly and kill any existing mold before you paint. Painting over active mold will not solve the problem; it will just hide it temporarily until it grows back through the new paint.

## What to Look for in Bathroom Ceiling Paint

The primary factor in choosing paint for a bathroom ceiling is its inherent mold and mildew resistance. This usually comes from anti-mold and mildew additives in the paint itself. Without these, even a durable paint will eventually succumb to the constant humidity.

Durability and ease of cleaning are also important. Bathroom ceilings are regularly exposed to steam and humidity, so the finish needs to withstand frequent wiping without damage. While flat finishes are common for ceilings because they hide imperfections, for bathrooms, a satin or semi-gloss finish often offers superior moisture resistance and washability. Some specialized matte finishes are also designed specifically for high-humidity environments.

## What to Skip

Skip any standard interior ceiling paint that doesn't explicitly mention mold or mildew resistance. These paints are not formulated to handle the constant humidity of a bathroom and will likely fail prematurely. Also, be wary of flat finishes in very humid bathrooms unless they are specifically designed for high-moisture environments, as they are generally harder to clean and less durable than higher sheens. Do not paint over existing mold; clean and treat the surface first.
