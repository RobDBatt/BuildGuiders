## What to look for

Two different things ruin a laminate floor in a house with animals, and they fail in different ways. Water gets into the core, the plank swells at the seam, and that damage is permanent. Claws scratch the wear layer, which is cosmetic until enough of them add up. Separate specs cover each, so check both.

On water, the word on the box is doing real work. "Waterproof" means the core itself won't absorb. "Water resistant" means the surface holds out for a while and the seams are where it gives. That is the difference between an accident you find in an hour and one you find the next morning.

On scratches, look for the Abrasion Class rating. AC4 is rated for heavy residential traffic and is where to start if a dog lives there. AC5 is commercial-grade and more floor than most houses need. AC3 and below will show claw marks inside a year.

Then there is texture, which almost nobody brings up. A smooth high-gloss plank is slippery under paws and shows every hair. An embossed finish gives some grip and hides the scratches that happen anyway.

## Shaw Repel

Shaw markets Repel as water *resistant*, not waterproof, and that distinction is the whole reason it sits last here. The core buys you time against a spill; it does not buy you a puddle that sat overnight. AC4, wide style range, and it costs less than the three above.

Buy it for a house with a cat and the occasional knocked-over water bowl. Do not buy it for a puppy being house-trained, or for a dog that drinks like a shop vac and walks away dripping.

## What to skip

Anything that won't say "waterproof" or back the claim with a water-damage warranty. Standard laminate has a wood-based core, and once that swells the plank does not go back — you are replacing boards, not cleaning them.

Skip AC1 through AC3 in a pet house. Those ratings exist for bedrooms and light traffic, and claws will find them.

And skip high-gloss, however good it looks in the showroom. It's slick under paws and it shows every mark you were trying to avoid.
