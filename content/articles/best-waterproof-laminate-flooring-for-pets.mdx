## What to Look for in Waterproof Laminate Flooring for Pets

Choosing the right waterproof laminate flooring for a home with pets requires focusing on specific features that address common challenges like accidents, scratches, and dander. The primary concern is true water resistance, meaning the flooring can withstand spills and pet accidents without swelling, warping, or allowing moisture to seep into the subfloor. Look for products with a waterproof core, often made from high-density fiberboard (HDF) treated with water-repellent resins or a rigid core material that inherently resists moisture. The locking system between planks is equally critical; a tight, secure click-lock mechanism prevents liquids from penetrating the seams, which is a common failure point for standard laminate.

Beyond water protection, durability is key. Pets, especially active ones, can be hard on floors. An Abrasion Class (AC) rating of AC4 or AC5 indicates high resistance to scratches, dents, and wear, making it suitable for high-traffic areas and homes with multiple or larger pets. Surface texture also plays a role in pet comfort and safety. While a smooth finish might seem easy to clean, it can be slippery for pets, leading to falls or discomfort. Opt for laminate with an embossed-in-register (EIR) texture or a subtle grain that provides better traction for paws while still being easy to wipe clean. Finally, consider the warranty; some manufacturers offer specific pet-friendly or water damage warranties, providing extra peace of mind.

## What to Skip

When selecting waterproof laminate for pets, it's wise to bypass any laminate flooring that doesn't explicitly state "waterproof" or "water-resistant" in its product description. Standard laminate, even if durable, will swell and delaminate when exposed to pet accidents or spills. Also, avoid products with low Abrasion Class (AC) ratings, such as AC1 or AC2, as these are not designed to withstand the scratching and impact from pet claws or the heavy foot traffic common in pet-friendly homes. Extremely smooth or high-gloss finishes, while visually appealing, can create a slippery surface for pets, leading to discomfort or injury. Finally, be wary of products with visibly loose or weak locking mechanisms, as these are prone to allowing moisture to seep into the subfloor, undermining any claims of water resistance.
