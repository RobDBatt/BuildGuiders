## What to Look for in Waterproof Laminate Flooring for Pets

When selecting laminate flooring for a home with pets, the primary concern is its ability to withstand spills, accidents, and daily wear. True waterproof laminate has a core that resists water absorption, preventing swelling and warping even if liquids sit on the surface for a period. This differs from water-resistant laminate, which typically only protects the surface from moisture for a shorter duration. Look for products that explicitly state a waterproof core or offer a warranty against water damage.

Beyond water protection, consider the flooring's durability against scratches and scuffs from claws. The Abrasion Class (AC) rating indicates a laminate's resistance to wear. For pet households, an AC4 rating is a good starting point, offering protection against heavy residential traffic and general abrasions. An AC5 rating provides even greater resilience. Surface texture also plays a role; a slightly textured or embossed finish can provide better traction for pets, reducing slips and falls compared to very smooth, glossy surfaces. This texture can also help camouflage minor scratches. Finally, ease of cleaning is important. Laminate with tight seams and a smooth, non-porous surface makes it simpler to wipe up pet hair, dander, and accidents.

## What to Skip

Avoid any laminate flooring that does not explicitly state "waterproof" or offer a robust water-damage warranty. Standard laminate, while durable against surface wear, uses a wood-based core that will swell and warp irreversibly if exposed to moisture from pet accidents or spills for too long. Also, skip laminate with a low AC rating (AC1, AC2, or AC3) in a pet household. These ratings indicate less resistance to abrasion and will show scratches and scuffs from pet claws much more quickly than an AC4 or AC5 product. Finally, overly smooth or high-gloss finishes, while visually appealing, can be slippery for pets and may highlight every piece of pet hair or paw print.
